The Ritual of the Painted Bride

A bride wears paint on her face to prevent bad luck during a traditional wedding ceremony in the village of Donje Ljubinje, Kosovo. The ritual takes over two hours, using white base, golden concentric circles representing life cycles, and protective dots shielding against envy and bad luck.

An Afar Wedding

Weddings among the Afar people of Djibouti

CD1-02
Djibouti • Afar Wedding p. 4

What is an Afar wedding ceremony like?

The actual wedding ceremony is only for men. The bride¹ does not go to this ceremony. Instead, her father represents² her. The women celebrate³ in a different place. They sit and talk about the bride’s life. The women’s ceremony often includes a lot of dancing.

What happens next?

After these separate ceremonies, the bride and groom⁴ meet with their families and friends. Sometimes this party lasts all night. The next day, the bride and groom invite their families to their new home. They eat a traditional⁵ meal. A typical meal is goat with rice.

What do the bride and groom wear?

Often they both dress in traditional clothes, but the bride sometimes wears a western-style dress. Some brides wear a lot of gold jewelry.

Who pays for the wedding?

In the Afar culture, the man pays for the wedding. He also provides a home for his new wife, but they do not live there together until three days after the wedding.

Footnotes & Context

¹ bride: a woman on her wedding day

² represent: to act in the place of someone

³ celebrate: to have a party or meal to mark a special occasion

⁴ groom: a man on his wedding day

⁵ traditional: based on customs practiced for a long time

Geographic Note: Many Afar people live in Africa’s Great Rift Valley.

Yes / No Questions & Short Answers

• Do I / you / we / they sing? → Yes, they do. / No, they don’t.

• Does he / she / it sing? → Yes, he does. / No, he doesn’t.

Wh- Questions (Object)

Wh- word + do/does + subject + base verb

• Where does Jeff live? (In Sydney.)

• Why do they sing? (They love music.)

Who / What as Subject

Who/What + 3rd person singular verb (-s/-es)

• Who teaches this class? (Prof. Ortega)

⚠️ Do NOT use do/does! (NOT: Who does teach?)

Chart 1.3 & Exercise 8: Frequency Adverbs

Rules of position with be and other verbs; rewriting 10 full sentences

Chart 1.3

1.3 Frequency Adverbs

Page 8
100% Always Usually Often Sometimes Rarely 0% Never

• Position with 'be': After be (I am sometimes early for class; Guests are usually happy).

• Position with other verbs: Before other verbs (I usually enjoy parties).

• Sometimes, usually, frequently, often can also begin or end a sentence.

• Ever: Common in questions (Do you ever eat out?). Not usually used in affirmative statements.
