Diwali (Festival of Lights)

Jaipur, Rajasthan, India

CD1-05
Audio Track • CD1-05
Jaipur, India • Diwali Celebration p. 12

What are these women doing?

These women in India are getting ready for Diwali. Diwali is an important holiday for Hindus. It is also called the Festival of Lights. Diwali happens in late October or early November every year.

What do Hindus do during Diwali?

People like to have new clothes for Diwali. The women in the photo are wearing new saris¹. They are lighting sparklers and placing them around their home. They want their family to have a successful future. The women hope that the sparklers attract Lakshmi, the Hindu goddess of wealth and success. The star-shaped design on the floor is a rangoli. It also helps to attract the goddess.

Hindus believe that Lakshmi visits people’s homes during Diwali. In the Hindu religion, a visit from Lakshmi is a very good thing. It means a happy future for the family.

Vocabulary & Visual Notes

¹ sari: a traditional form of dress for women in India and other South Asian countries.

Rangoli: Intricate patterns created on the floor using colored sand, rice, or flowers.

Photograph context: Women lighting sparklers for Diwali in Jaipur, Rajasthan, India.

Chart 1.6 & Exercise 8: Action vs. Non-Action Verbs

Understanding stative verbs and when non-action verbs change meaning in progressive

Chart 1.6

1.6 Action and Non-Action (Stative) Verbs

Page 16

Non-action verbs describe states, feelings, or senses. They are not usually used in progressive (✓ They own a house; ✗ They are owning).

Feelings dislike, hate, like, love, miss
Senses feel, hear, see, smell, sound, taste
Possession belong, have, own
Appearance appear, look, seem
Desires hope, need, prefer, want
Mental believe, think, understand
Meaning Shift with Progressive:

• have: He has a headache (state) vs He is having lunch (action)

• think: I think this book is great (opinion) vs I am thinking of the answer (mental process)

• taste: The food tastes good (property) vs She is tasting the food (action)

12 Venice Carnival Analysis (Page 19)

Model Analysis

Notice simple present verbs and frequency adverbs in this cultural text:

"I come from Venice, Italy, and I really love my city. My favorite time of year is Carnival. People wear masks and beautiful costumes and parade through the streets. My family and I always have a little competition to see who has the best costume. My sister is in art school, so she usually wins. The rest of us compete for second prize!"
Key Points Noticed:

• come, love, wear, parade, have, wins, compete: Simple present describing recurring habits and feelings.

• always, usually: Frequency adverbs positioned before regular verbs.
