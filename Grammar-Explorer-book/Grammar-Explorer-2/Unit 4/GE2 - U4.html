4.1 Subject and Object Pronouns

Page 89
Subject & Object Pronouns Forms & Roles
Subject Pronoun + Verb Verb + Object Pronoun
I / You arrived late. John visited me / you.
He / She / It arrived late. John visited him / her / it.
We / You / They arrived late. John visited us / you / them.

1. Subject Pronouns: A pronoun takes the place of a noun or noun phrase. Subject pronouns are used as subjects:

• Elena enjoys driving. She uses her car a lot.

• Tom and I went on vacation together. We went to Portugal.

• Your suitcase arrived. It’s in your room.

2. Object Pronouns: Object pronouns are used as objects of verbs or objects of prepositions:

• Object of Verb: I bought a new bike. Do you like it?

• Object of Preposition: This ticket is for José. I bought it for him.

4.2 Possessive Adjectives & Pronouns

Page 91
Possessive Forms Adjective vs. Pronoun
Possessive Adjective + Noun Possessive Pronoun (Alone)
My / Your coat is black. This coat is mine / yours.
His / Her / Its coat is black. This coat is his / hers / its.
Our / Your / Their coat is black. This coat is ours / yours / theirs.

1. Possessive Adjectives: Use before a noun to show ownership (Joe bought his plane ticket yesterday. The dog pushed its bowl across the floor.).

2. Possessive Pronouns: Use in place of a possessive adjective + noun (You can use my bike. Mine is the blue one. That’s not your coat. Yours is in the closet.).

3. Roles of Possessive Pronouns:

• Subject: Where are the bikes? His is outside. Hers is downstairs.

• Object of Verb: I found their keys, but I can’t find ours.

• Object of Preposition: Our car is nice, but look at theirs.

⚠️ Be Careful! Sound-Alikes

• Its vs. It’s: Its is a possessive adjective (The team won its first game.). It’s is the contraction of it is or it has (It’s on the table.).

• Their vs. There vs. They’re: Their is a possessive adjective (They found their coats.). There refers to a place (There are two coats here.). They’re is the contraction of they are (They’re on the chair.).

4.3 Reflexive Pronouns

Page 93
Reflexive Pronoun Forms Singular ➔ Plural
Singular (-self) I ➔ myself, you ➔ yourself, he ➔ himself, she ➔ herself, it ➔ itself
Plural (-selves) we ➔ ourselves, you ➔ yourselves, they ➔ themselves

1. Subject and Object Are the Same: Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and the object of the verb are the same person or thing:

✓ I introduced myself to the group.

✗ I introduced me to the group.

2. Common Verbs with Reflexives: Verbs frequently followed by reflexive pronouns include:

• cut, enjoy, feel sorry for, hurt, take care of, tell

Did you cut yourself on the broken glass? / We enjoyed ourselves at the party.

3. By + Reflexive Pronoun: Means alone or without any help:

• I live by myself. (= alone)

• The TV turns off by itself. (= without help)

Diving in the Bahamas

Exploring underwater blue holes and marine environments

CD1-34
Dean's Blue Hole & Caves • Bahamas p. 96

For many years, the Bahamas, a group of islands in the Atlantic, has been a popular vacation spot. The islands attract a lot of divers. Divers from many countries come to see the wide variety of fish in the islands’ clear waters.

The fish differ from season to season. Spring is the best time to see mahi-mahi. In October, manta rays are more common. In the winter, divers may even see hammerhead sharks.

For experienced divers, the blue holes of the Bahamas are especially interesting. From above, these blue holes look like deep blue ponds. In fact, they are entrances to underwater caves. There are over 1000 of these blue holes in the Bahamas. They are one of the most unusual environments on Earth. At the surface, there is a thin layer of fresh rainwater. Below that, there is salt water because the caves open to the sea.

4.4 Prepositions of Time

Page 97

1. Prepositional Phrases: A preposition is usually part of a phrase with its object (a noun or noun phrase): Our train leaves in the morning.

2. Prepositions of Time: Tell when something happens: She took her vacation in the summer. On Sunday, he slept late.

3. at: Clock times and night: at nine o’clock, at noon, at night, at midnight.

4. in: Periods of time, including parts of a day (except night), seasons, months, and years: in the morning, in the summer, in September, in 1999.

5. on: Dates and days of the week: on July 15, on Sunday, on weekdays.

6. Other Common Prepositions of Time:

• for (duration): We took lessons for three years.

• from . . . to/until: Stefan studies from 6:00 until 10:00.

• during: She likes to talk during class.

4.5 Prepositions of Place and Direction

Page 99

1. Prepositions of Place: Tell where someone or something is: near the window, behind the restaurant, outside the cafeteria, under the bridge, across the room.

2. in:

• Inside a container: Put the book in a box. I left the keys in the car.

• Countries, states, and cities: He lives in China. They have an apartment in Shanghai.

3. on:

• Position on a surface: Put the book on the shelf. I left the keys on the table.

• Names of streets: The store is on Fifth Avenue. I got lost on Brookline Street.

4. at:

• Specific point: Go left at the stop sign.

• Addresses: My brother lives at 29 School Street.

5. Prepositions of Direction: Tell which way someone or something is going: We walked toward the post office. I drove from Portland to Seattle. I saw Mario between the buildings.

Real English: No Articles with Common Prepositional Phrases

A few common phrases do not take a/an/the before the noun:

• in class, in bed, at home, at school, at work

e.g., I stayed in bed until 11:00 a.m. / I’ll call him later. He’s at work now.

4.6 Indefinite and Definite Articles

Page 105
Articles Overview Indefinite vs. Definite
Indefinite (A, An, Some, Ø) Definite (The)
Singular Count: She needs a coat. He has an idea. We looked at the map.
Plural Count: We bought some peaches. I like Ø apples. She opened the packages.
Non-Count: She doesn’t drink Ø tea. I washed the fruit.

1. Indefinite Article (a / an): Use with singular count nouns that are not specific (She is at a party. Where can I buy an umbrella?).

• Sound Rule: Use a before consonant sounds (a college, a university); use an before vowel sounds (an adventure, an important point).

2. some or No Article (Ø): Use before plural count nouns or non-count nouns for non-specific items (There are some motorcycles in the parking lot. / I need to buy some gas. / He likes motorcycles.).

3. Definite Article (the): Use to talk about a specific person, place, or thing. Use the when:

• There is only one: Why are you looking at the sun?

• Speaker and listener share context: Did you feed the baby?

• Already mentioned: There’s a woman with a child. The child is laughing.

4.7 Generalizations vs. Specific References

Page 107
Generalization vs. Specific All members vs. Particular
Generalization Specific Reference
Singular: A bike is a cheap form of transport. The orange bike is my sister’s.
Plural: Bikes don’t use any gas. The bikes are in the garage.

1. Generalization Defined: A generalization is a statement about all members of a group: A city is a great place to live. / Cities are great places to live.

2. a / an + Singular Count Noun: Use to make a generalization with a singular count noun: A car is the best way to get around. An icy street is dangerous.

3. No Article (Ø) + Non-Count or Plural: Use to make a generalization: Chocolate is delicious. Vacations are relaxing.

4. Specific References with the: Always use the when pointing to particular items: The restaurant on Bay Street is popular. The people at my office are friendly.

4.8 The and No Article: Geographic Names

Page 111
The vs. No Article (Ø) Geographic Names
The Baghdad is on the Tigris River. They live in the United States.
No Article (Ø) She is from Brazil. Africa is a huge continent.

1. Cities, States, Countries, Continents (Ø): Use no article before most: Barcelona, Texas, Morocco, Africa.

2. of- phrase, Kingdom, Republic (The): Use the: the District of Columbia, the United Kingdom, the Dominican Republic.

3. Plural Geographic Names (The): Use the: the United States, the Philippines, the Alps, the Andes, the Hawaiian Islands.

4. Single Mountains & Single Islands (Ø): Do not use the: Mount Everest, Mount McKinley, Long Island, Maui.

5. Oceans, Seas, and Rivers (The): Use the (the word ocean/sea/river is often omitted): the Caspian Sea, the Amazon, the Atlantic Ocean.

6. Deserts (The): Use the (word desert often omitted): the Mojave Desert, the Sahara.
