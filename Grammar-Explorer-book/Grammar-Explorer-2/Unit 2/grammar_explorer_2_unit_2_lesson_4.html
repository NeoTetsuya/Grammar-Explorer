Who Owns the Past?

British Museum, London: The Parthenon Marbles

CD1-13
Parthenon Statues • Acropolis, Athens pp. 46–47

Cho: These statues are beautiful. Where are they from?

Noriko: They’re from Athens, Greece. I think they used to be a part of the Parthenon.

Cho: Well, it’s wonderful to see them, but why are they here in London? They should be back in Greece with the rest of the Parthenon.

Noriko: A lot of people agree with you. Lord Elgin, a British diplomat,¹ brought these statues to London in the early nineteenth century.

Cho: You mean he just took them?

Noriko: Well, I don’t know. I’m not sure what really happened. Back then archaeologists² used to do things like that. They would explore a historic site and bring objects from the site back to their home countries. Then, they would study them and put them on display in museums. Sometimes, that’s the only reason treasures like these survived.

Cho: But didn’t the Greek people try to stop Lord Elgin?

Noriko: Sure, there were a lot of complaints. Was it right or wrong to take these statues? It’s a difficult question. I love to visit museums and see things from around the world and learn about them.

Cho: Hmm . . . I understand what you’re saying, but it still seems wrong to me, somehow.

¹ diplomat: a person who acts as the official representative of his or her country in a foreign country

² archaeologist: a person who studies the past by looking at items such as buried houses, tools, pots, etc.

Chart 2.7 & Exercise 4: Used To Statements

Expressing past habits or states that no longer exist; forming negatives with didn't use to

Chart 2.7

2.7 Used To: Affirmative & Negative

Page 48
Affirmative (used to + base form):

• I / She / They used to study history.

Meaning: In the past, but not anymore.

Negative (didn't use to + base form):

• I / She / They didn't use to study math.

Meaning: Did not happen in the past, but happens now.

Spelling Guardrail:

In negative statements, write didn’t use to without the final d.

✓ I didn't use to love math.

✗ I didn't used to love math.

Chart 2.9

2.9 Would: Repeated Past Actions

Page 50
Affirmative & Negative:

• When I was in high school, I would ride my bike to school.

• When Scott was a child, he would not eat any vegetables.

Critical Distinction: Actions vs. States

Do not use would for past situations or states that no longer exist. Use used to instead!

✓ There used to be a library on this street.

✗ There would be a library on this street.
