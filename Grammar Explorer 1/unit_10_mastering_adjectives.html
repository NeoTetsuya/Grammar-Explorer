1

Modifying Nouns with Adjectives

Adjectives describe or modify nouns. They appear in two main patterns:

After the verb 'be'

Modifies the subject directly.

The desert is hot. modifies
The foxes are small. modifies

Before a noun

Placed directly in front of the noun.

It's an interesting article. modifies
He is a tall man. modifies

Core Rules

  • Invariant Form: Adjectives NEVER change form for plural nouns.
    The rooms are big. | The rooms are bigs.
  • Indefinite Articles (a/an): Use "a" before consonant sounds (a good student). Use "an" before vowel sounds (an excellent teacher).

Prepositions & Intensifiers

  • Fixed Prepositions: afraid of, worried about, good for.
  • Intensifiers: Use "very" or "really" before adjectives. "Really" is less formal.
2

Questions: How vs What

  • How + Adjective

    Ask about measurements: How old are you?

  • What + Be + Subject + Like

    Ask for general descriptions: What's she like?

  • What + Do/Does + Subj + Like

    Ask for hobbies/preferences: What does she like?

3

Nouns as Adjectives

Nouns can modify other nouns.

Singular Form Rule

The modifying noun MUST remain singular.

horse trainer
horses trainer

You can also use nouns ending in -ing: camping trip.

4

Adjectives vs. Adverbs of Manner

Core Differences

  • Adjectives modify nouns/pronouns.
    "I am careful."
  • Adverbs describe action verbs (how an action is done).
    "I drive carefully."

Form & Placement

  • Formed by adding -ly (beautifully, slowly).
  • Irregular: good → well.
  • Never place an adverb between an action verb and its direct object.
    I carefully drove the car.
    I drove carefully the car.
5

Linking Verbs + Adjectives

Linking verbs connect the subject to an adjective describing it: be, become, feel, get, look, seem, smell, sound, stay, taste.

Linking: The flower smells good. (Adjective)
Action: I smelled the flower carefully. (Adverb)

Note: Adjectives like afraid, asleep, alone, awake must only be used after the verb 'be' (e.g., The baby is asleep).

6

Identical Forms & Traps

Some words have the same form as both adjectives and adverbs: early, fast, hard, late.

  • Adj: He's a fast runner.
  • Adv: He runs fast.
Don't be fooled! Words like friendly, lonely, ugly end in -ly but are adjectives. Formal English requires slowly as the adverb.

7. Too, Very, and Enough

Too

Means "more than necessary". It indicates a negative result or problem.

The hotel is too noisy.
(Result: We can't sleep)

Very

A neutral intensifier. It makes the meaning stronger without implying a negative problem.

The people were very friendly.

Enough

Means "to a satisfactory extent". MUST come AFTER the adjective or adverb.

This room is big enough.
enough big
Often followed by an infinitive: strong enough to lift.
