Information Questions

With the Verb "Be"

No 'do/does/did' needed!

Rule: Invert Subject and Be

When be is the main verb, simply move it before the subject.

  • Present (am/is/are):
    Where is the library?
  • Past (was/were):
    When were you in Norway?
Contraction Rule: You can contract Where is to Where's, but never contract Where are in writing.

With Other Verbs

You MUST use an auxiliary verb.

Rule: Add Do / Does / Did

For all verbs except "be", you need a helping verb. The main verb reverts to the base form.

  • Simple Present:
    Why does he live in Miami?
  • Simple Past:
    What did she buy?
  • Progressive Tenses:
    Where are they going?

The Big Rule: Subject vs. Object Questions

When using Who or What, the grammar changes completely depending on whether the Wh- word is the doer of the action (Subject) or the receiver (Object).

Subject Questions

Asking about the doer. DO NOT use do/does/did! Conjugate the verb directly as a 3rd-person singular.

Present Who knows the answer?
Past What happened to you?

Object Questions

Asking about the receiver. Normal rules apply. You MUST use an auxiliary verb (do/does/did) and a subject.

Present Who do you see?
Past What did they do?

Tricky Question Words

What ... like? (Description)

Uses "be" to ask for a description. Do not confuse with "What does she like?" (preferences).

"What is she like?" → "She is nice."

Whose vs. Who's

Whose asks about possession. Who's means "Who is".

"Whose car is this?" (Possession)

"Who's your teacher?" (Identity)

How much / How many

  • How much: Non-count nouns (water, time, homework).
  • How many: Plural count nouns (apples, people, books).

Modals & Conversation

Questions with Modals

Modals (can, should, would) act like auxiliaries. Place them before the subject.

Where can we get coffee?

What should I bring?

Polite Requests

Use Could you or Would you before the subject to be polite.

"Could you open the door?"

Conversational Rule

In speech, answers to Wh- questions are rarely full sentences, and Wh- words can be used alone to ask for clarification.

"I only sleep 4 hours." → "Why?"
