Grammar Quick Reference Rules

Simple Present vs. Present Progressive

Simple Present is used for permanent habits, facts, and routines (e.g., I work every day). Present Progressive is for actions happening right now or temporary situations (e.g., I am working today).

Count vs. Non-Count Nouns

Count nouns take plural -s (apples, books). Non-count nouns do not take plural -s and use singular verb forms (information, water, advice, rice).

Non-Action / Stative Verbs Rule

Verbs describing mental states, feelings, or possession (know, love, need, belong, want) are NOT usually used in progressive tenses (-ing).

Past Interrupted Actions (When / While)

Use Past Progressive for the longer background action and Simple Past for the interrupting event: While I was studying, the phone rang.
