1. Comparative Adjectives

Comparing TWO people, places, or things.

The Grand Hotel is nicer than the Plaza.
Always use than after a comparative!

Spelling Rules

Short (1 syllable): Add -er
high → higher
Silent -e: Add -r
large → larger
CVC (Short): Double + -er
big → bigger, hot → hotter
Consonant + y: -ier
heavy → heavier
Long (2+ syllables): more + adj
famous → more famous, difficult → more difficult

Using "One / Ones"

Use to avoid repeating the noun.

This house is older than that one.

2. Superlative Adjectives

Comparing THREE OR MORE items.

Mount Everest is the highest mountain.
Always use the before a superlative!

Spelling Rules

Short: Add -est
old → oldest
Silent -e: Add -st
nice → nicest
CVC: Double + -est
thin → thinnest
Consonant + y: -iest
easy → easiest
Long: the most + adj
modern → the most modern

One of the...

Followed by a plural noun.

One of the best restaurants.

Irregulars

  • good → best
  • bad → worst
  • far → farthest

3. Possessive Pronouns

Used in place of a possessive adjective + noun to avoid repetition.

Adjective + Noun
Pronoun
my book
mine
your book
yours
his/her book
his/hers
our/their book
ours/theirs

The Golden Rule

Do not place a noun directly after a possessive pronoun.

The book is mine.
This is mine book.

4. Questions with Whose

Ask about ownership using Whose + noun.

Asking about an Object Whose car did you take?
Asking about a Subject Whose car goes faster?

Whose vs. Who's

They sound identical but mean very different things.

  • Whose indicates possession.
    "Whose book is this?"
  • Who's is short for 'Who is'.
    "Who's at the door?"
