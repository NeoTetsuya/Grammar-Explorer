1. Count vs. Non-Count

Count Nouns

Things you can count. Have singular & plural forms.

one apple → two apples

Non-Count Nouns

Cannot be counted. No plural form.

sugar sugars

Categories of Non-Count Nouns

Foods: bread, meat, pasta, rice Liquids: coffee, oil, water, soup Activities: soccer, tennis, yoga Categories: furniture, luggage, money, weather Abstract: advice, fun, information, work

Dual-Meaning Nouns

Some words change depending on context.

  • Non-Count (Substance): I love pizza.
  • Count (Whole Item): We ordered three pizzas.

2. Articles & Measurements

Articles (A / An / The)

  • A / An (Indefinite) Use ONLY with singular count nouns.
    She has a suitcase. Consonant sound

    He ate an orange. Vowel sound
    *Exceptions: a university (sounds like 'y'), an hour (silent 'h').
  • The (Definite) Can be used with BOTH count and non-count nouns when specific or already known (e.g., the earth, the water on the table).

Measurement Words

Used with 'of' to count non-count nouns.

RULE
I bought two cartons of eggs. Make the measurement plural!
two carton of eggs

Common: a bottle of, a slice of, a piece of, a loaf of, a bowl of.

3. Quantifiers

Much, Many, A lot of, A few, A little

Quantifier Used With Example
Many Count (Plural) I have many apples.
Much Non-Count I don't have much time.
A lot of / Lots of Count Non-Count We eat a lot of vegetables & honey.
A few Count (Plural) I have a few pencils.
A little Non-Count Add a little milk.

4. Some vs. Any

SOME

  • Affirmative statements.
  • Polite offers (Would you like some...?).

ANY

  • Negative statements.
  • General questions (Do you have any...?).

5. How much vs. How many

How much money does it cost? Use with Non-Count
How many bottles do we need? Use with Plural Count

Note: You can omit the noun in conversation if it's obvious! ("How many do we need?")
