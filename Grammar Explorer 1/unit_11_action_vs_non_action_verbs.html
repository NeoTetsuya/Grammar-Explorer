Unit 11 Verbs

Grammar: Action & Non-Action Verbs

Learn the difference between verbs you do and verbs you feel or know, and how to use them in sentences correctly.

Interactive: Tap or hover over dashed words to see grammar rules in action!
1

Action Verbs

An action verb describes a physical action or something you actively choose to do. Because they are actions, they can be used in progressive tenses (like present continuous with -ing).

He drives to work every day. Habit/Routine
They are leaving right now. Happening Now (-ing)
Critical Rule
2

Non-Action Verbs (The Progressive Rule)

A non-action verb describes situations, feelings, or conditions (not physical actions).

The Rule: Non-action verbs cannot be used in progressive (-ing) tenses. You must use the simple present or simple past instead.

Correct Usage

I know the answer.
We saw Gemma yesterday.

Incorrect Usage

I am knowing the answer.
We were seeing Gemma.

5 Categories of Non-Action Verbs:

Emotions
She loves her children.
(like, hate, prefer) No -ing!
Thoughts
Kim knows the answer.
(understand, think, believe) No -ing!
Possessions
He owns a car.
(belong, have, possess) No -ing!
States of Being
We are happy.
(be, seem, exist) No -ing!
Senses
I see Tanya.
(hear, smell, taste) No -ing!
3

Active vs. Automatic Senses

Senses can be divided into actions you choose to do, and things that happen to you automatically.

Automatic = NON-ACTION

Happens without control. (No -ing)

  • I see the train.
  • I hear a noise.

Chosen = ACTION

You actively focus. (-ing is OK!)

  • I'm watching a movie.
  • We're listening to her.
4

Verbs with Dual Meanings

Some verbs, like "Have", change their rules depending on their meaning in the sentence.

Meaning: Possession (Non-Action)
He has a car. Ownership = No -ing
Meaning: Activity (Action)
He is having lunch right now. Eating = Action (-ing OK)
