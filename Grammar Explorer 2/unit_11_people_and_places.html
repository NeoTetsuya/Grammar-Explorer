Relative Clauses

Combine ideas effortlessly! Use adjective clauses to describe people, places, and things as subjects, objects, or possessives.

Tap the dashed grammar words in the examples to reveal their rules!

1. Subject Relative Clauses

In a subject relative clause, the relative pronoun acts as the subject of the clause and is followed directly by a verb.

Positions & Pronouns

  • After Main Clause: Subject + Verb + Noun + [who/that/which + Verb].
    "We met the woman who owns the shop."
  • Inside Main Clause: Subject + [who/that/which + Verb] + Main Verb.
    "The tour that left at noon was great."
  • Pronoun Choice: Who / That for people.
    Which / That for places, things, and animals.
    *Real English: "That" is the most common in conversation.

Verb Agreement & Constraint

Verb Agreement Rule: The verb inside the relative clause must agree with the noun it describes.
Singular Noun: I have a friend who likes traveling.
Plural Noun: I have friends who like traveling.
No Subject Repetition!

Never repeat the subject pronoun inside the subject relative clause.

Where's the book that it has the map?
Where's the book that has the map?

2. Object Relative Clauses & Pronoun Omission

In an object relative clause, the relative pronoun is the object of the clause and is followed by a subject and a verb.

Structure & Pronouns

  • Pronoun Choices: Use who, whom, that (people) or which, that (things/places).
    "He is the doctor that you saw last week."
  • Verb Agreement inside Clause: The verb inside agrees with its own subject, not the modified noun!
    "I like the work that she does." / "I like the work that they do."

Omission Rule & Errors

Omission in Conversation You can drop the object relative pronoun in informal English!
"She is wearing a ring that I like." → "She is wearing a ring I like." *Constraint: Never drop relative pronouns in SUBJECT clauses!
No Object Repetition!

Do not repeat the object pronoun (it, him, them) at the end of the relative clause.

She has a job that she loves it.
She has a job that she loves.

3. Prepositions & Clauses with "Whose"

Relative Clauses with Prepositions

Informal Word Order (End of Clause): Preposition comes after the verb. Pronoun can be omitted.
"The people [that] I met with were customers."
Formal Word Order (Front of Clause) Place preposition directly before whom (people) or which (things).
"The person to whom we spoke was helpful."
Never place prepositions before who or that! (e.g., to who, for that).

Possessive Clauses with "Whose"

Whose + Noun Rule: Replaces possessive adjectives (his, her, my, their, Sam's). A noun must always follow whose!
"That's the bakery whose cakes we love."
Syntactic Roles: Subject: The author whose book won the award... Object: She's the author whose book I bought...
No Double Possession / Repetition! The artist whose paintings they are in the gallery. The artist whose paintings are in the gallery.

Writing Focus: Transition Words in a Classification Essay

To lead readers smoothly from one categorized group of ideas to the next in a classification essay, use sequential transition expressions:

The first group of cures I learned about is made up of drinks. The second group of treatments consists of foods. The last group involves physical activities.
