1. Gerunds (Verb + -ing)

A gerund is the base verb + -ing functioning as a noun. It can serve as a subject or direct object.

Subjects & Objects

  • Subject Agreement (Always Singular) A gerund or gerund phrase subject demands a 3rd-person singular verb.
    "Making new friends takes time."
  • Negative Form Place not directly before the gerund.
    "We considered not going to the party."
  • Go + Gerund (Recreation) Used for sports and leisure activities:
    go camping, go hiking, go skiing, go dancing, go shopping, go swimming, go running.

Objects of Prepositions

A gerund is required whenever a verb follows a preposition!

Noun + Preposition choice between, need for, in danger of, reason for, benefit of, cause of, interest in, problem with.
Verb + Preposition believe in, succeed in, dream about/of, talk about, feel like, think about, plan on/for, worry about, apologize for, insist on, thank for.
Be + Adjective + Preposition afraid of, nervous about, excited about, good at, tired of, interested in, accustomed to, bad at, famous for, sick of, used to.
By + Gerund (Method) "I learned to fix my bike by watching a video."

Verbs Followed by Gerunds

admit, advise, anticipate, appreciate, avoid, can't help, complete, consider, delay, deny, detest, discuss, dislike, enjoy, finish, forbid, imagine, keep, mention, mind, miss, permit, postpone, practice, put off, quit, recall, recommend, regret, remember, resent, resist, risk, stop, suggest, tolerate, understand.

2. Infinitives & Combined Patterns

Verb (+ Object) + Infinitive

  • Direct Verb + Infinitive "They decided to take the bus."
    Verbs: agree, expect, need, refuse, appear, forget, plan, seem, ask, hope, pretend, want, decide, learn, promise, would like.
  • Verb + Object + Infinitive An object is required before the infinitive:
    "We invited our friends to go."
    Verbs: advise, allow, teach, invite, order, tell, encourage, remind, warn, convince, enable, force, persuade, require, urge.
  • Polite Invitations "Would you like to go to the movies?"

Dual Patterns & Series Rules

Subject vs. Object Performer Shift Verbs like ask, help, pay, want, expect, need, promise, choose, would like shift meaning based on object presence:
No Object (Subject performs): "We expect to be there."
With Object (Object performs): "We expect her to be there."
Verbs with NO Difference in Meaning begin, continue, like, prefer, can't stand, hate, love, start, (not) bother.
"I like to swim" = "I like swimming."

Critical Constraint: Parallel Series Matching

When connecting multiple verbs with and or or, keep all forms identical! Do not mix gerunds and infinitives in the same series, and do not repeat to before subsequent infinitives.

She loves to cook and traveling.
She loves to cook and travel.

3. Infinitives of Purpose, Too & Enough

Infinitives of Purpose

Explains why an action occurred.

"I went to the store to buy food."
Common Error Never say for to find. Use the infinitive alone!

Too + Infinitive

Explains why an action is not possible.

  • too + adj/adv + inf:
    "Jason is too short to reach the shelf."
  • too much/many + noun + inf:
    "I have too much work to do."

Enough + Infinitive

Explains why an action is possible.

  • adj/adv + ENOUGH + inf:
    "I am strong enough to lift it."
  • ENOUGH + noun + inf:
    "She has enough talent to win."
Specifying "Who" with For + Object Insert for + noun/pronoun before the infinitive to specify who is affected:
"The screen is large enough for everyone to see it."
Enough Position Rule Place enough AFTER adjectives/adverbs, but BEFORE nouns!

Writing Focus: Sequencing with First and Then

When writing sequential descriptions (such as a biography or process), place a comma immediately after the transition words First and Then when starting a sentence/clause block.

First, he chopped vegetables and stirred beans. Then, he learned how to make delicious soups.
