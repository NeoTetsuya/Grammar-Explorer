1. Pronouns, Possessives & Reflexives

Subjects, Objects, Standalone Possessives, Reflexives & Reciprocals

Subject vs. Object vs. Possessive Pronouns

• Subject Pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they (→ She arrived at the airport.)
• Object Pronouns: me, you, him, her, it, us, them (→ The tour guide helped her.)
• Possessive Adjective (+ Noun): my, your, his, her, its, our, their ("her passport").
• Possessive Pronoun (Standalone): mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs (Mine is the blue suitcase).

Reflexive & Reciprocal Pronouns

• Reflexive Pronouns: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.
• Emphatic Use: "The captain himself checked the flight plan."
• By + Reflexive: Means alone / unassisted ("She traveled by herself.").
• Reciprocal Pronouns: each other (2 people) vs. one another (> 2 people).

Common Pronoun Traps

• its = possessive adjective | it's = contraction (it is / it has)
• their = possessive | there = place | they're = they are
• Indefinite pronouns (*everyone, somebody, nobody*) take singular verbs (*"Everyone is ready"*).

2. Prepositions of Time, Place & Movement

At, On, In, By, Until & Zero Article Expressions

Prepositions of Time & Deadlines

• At: exact clock times, at night, at noon, at midnight, at the weekend.
• In: morning/afternoon/evening, seasons, months, years (in July, in 2025).
• On: dates, days of week (on Monday, on July 15).
• By vs. Until: by = deadline (at or before time limit); until = continuous action up to moment.

Prepositions of Place & Movement

• In: enclosed spaces, cities, countries (in London, in a taxi).
• On: surfaces, streets, public transport (on Fifth Ave, on a train/bus/plane).
• At: specific address, point, event (at 29 Main Street, at the station, at a conference).
• Movement: into, onto, out of, off, through, across, towards, along.
Zero Article Place Phrases: in class, in bed, at home, at school, at work, by train/plane/bus/car.

3. Articles Matrix & Generalizations

A, An, The, or Zero Article (Ø)

Indefinite vs. Definite Rules

• A / An: First mention singular count noun (a hotel, an elevator).
• The: Specific/known item, second mention, superlatives, ordinals (the first flight, the most famous museum).

Generalizations vs. Specific References

• Generalizations: Use a/an for singular count (A bicycle is eco-friendly); use Ø (no article) for plural count or non-count (Bicycles are eco-friendly / Travel broadens the mind).
• Specific References: Use the (The bicycles parked outside belong to us).

4. Geographic Place Names & Writing

Rules with Places & Fixing Misplaced Phrases

No Article (Ø) • Continents, Countries, States, Cities (Asia, Japan, Tokyo).
• Single Mountains & Islands (Mount Fuji, Bali).
• Streets, Parks, Airports (Oxford St, Heathrow Airport).
Use "The" • Plural Names or "Republic/Kingdom/of" (the UK, the Netherlands).
• Oceans, Seas, Rivers, Deserts (the Pacific, the Nile).
• Hotels, Museums, Bridges (the Hilton, the British Museum).
Writing Focus: Misplaced Prepositional Phrases

Always place prepositional phrases right next to the exact word they modify!

Misplaced: We waited for the train inside the station with red doors.
Correct: Inside the station, we waited for the train with red doors.
