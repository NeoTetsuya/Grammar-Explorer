1. Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

Transitive vs. Intransitive

  • Transitive Verbs (Take Direct Object): Followed directly by a noun/pronoun experiencing the action (answers who or what).
    "Jon lost his car keys."
  • Intransitive Verbs (No Object): Cannot take a direct object. Followed by adverbs or prepositional phrases.
    "We arrived after dark."
    Common: arrive, fall, live, sleep, come, go, travel, wait.

Dual-Nature Verbs

Many verbs can be either transitive or intransitive depending on the context:

Dual Verbs: begin, close, open, call, continue, start, change, drive, stop.
Transitive Use: "He is driving his car."
"He opened the door."
Intransitive Use: "She drives to work."
"The doors open automatically."

Direct & Indirect Objects with To and For

Verbs taking "To" explain, give, offer, send, show, tell "He sent an e-mail to his parents."
Verbs taking "For" buy, fix, keep, make, get, provide "I bought a gift for my brother."
Either "To" or "For" bring, take "Bring a book to/for me."
Word Order & Pronoun Constraints
1. Omitting Prepositions (IO before DO) You can omit to/for by placing the Indirect Object directly before the Direct Object:
• With prep: We bought batteries for Timothy. • Without prep: We bought Timothy batteries.
2. Preposition Placement Restriction When using to/for, the phrase MUST come after the Direct Object!
He brought flowers to my mother. He brought to my mother flowers.
3. Indirect Object Pronoun If IO is a pronoun (me, him, us), it goes before DO with preposition omitted:
• "My cousin owes me money."
4. Direct Object Pronoun Constraint! If DO is a pronoun (it, them), it MUST come before IO, and you CANNOT omit to/for!
She got them for her sister. She got her sister them.

2. Phrasal Verbs: Separable vs. Inseparable

What is a Phrasal Verb?

A phrasal verb consists of a verb + particle (on, off, in, up, out, over). Particles look like prepositions but change the verb's meaning completely!

Phrasal Verb (Idiomatic): "Look out! There's a dog in the road!"
(Meaning: Be careful!)
Verb + Preposition (Literal): "I looked out the window."
(Literal directional sight)

Single-Word Equivalents

call off = cancel
find out = discover
get ahead = succeed
give up = quit
go over = review
hand in = submit
run into = meet by chance
talk over = discuss

Separable Phrasal Verbs

call off, find out, look up, set up, figure out, hand in, put on, talk over

• DO after: "I turned off the light."
• DO between: "I turned the light off."
Separable Pronoun Rule! When DO is a pronoun (it, them, him, her, me), it MUST go between the verb and particle!
She didn't pick it out.
She didn't pick out it.

Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

call on, come up with, go over, run into, come from, get on, look into, think about

• DO must ALWAYS follow particle!
I ran into an old friend.
I ran an old friend into.
Inseparable Pronoun Rule! Even if DO is a pronoun, it STILL MUST follow the particle! (3-word phrasal verbs like look up to, come up with are always inseparable too).
I came across it yesterday.
I came it across yesterday.

Writing Focus: Choosing an Effective Title

When writing an essay about an innovative product, phrasing your title as a compelling question is a powerful technique!

Catch Interest Make the title intriguing to grab the reader's attention immediately.
Capitalization Always begin with a capital letter and capitalize major words.
No Period at End Do NOT put a period at the end! End with a question mark (?) or exclamation mark (!) for emphasis.
