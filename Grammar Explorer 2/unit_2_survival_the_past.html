Simple Past: Statements, Forms & Subject Questions

Completed Events • States • Subject Questions

When to Use the Simple Past

  • Completed Actions in the Past: "In 2006, Maasai cattle herders in Kenya had a problem."
  • Repeated Actions in the Past: "Lions regularly killed their cows."
  • Past States & Feelings: "The Maasai were a big threat to the survival of lions."
Type Subject Verb / Auxiliary Example
Affirmative (Regular) All subjects verb + -ed / -d "They helped train Maasai warriors."
Affirmative (Irregular) All subjects irregular past form "The herders sent warriors to hunt."
Negative (Verbs) All subjects did not / didn't + base "The warriors didn’t kill any more lions."
Verb "BE" I / He / She / It
You / We / They		 was / wasn't
were / weren't		 "Herders weren't able to protect cows."
Be careful with "BE": Never use didn't with was or were!
✗ I didn’t was late for class yesterday. → ✓ I wasn’t late for class yesterday.

Questions & Subject Questions

Yes / No Questions & Short Answers

Did + Subject + Base Form?

"Did the whale survive?"

→ Yes, it did. / No, it didn’t.

"Did you finish your assignment?"

→ Yes, I did. / No, I didn’t.

Crucial Exam Focus Object Questions vs. Subject Questions
1. Object Wh- Questions (asking about object/time/reason):

Wh- Word + did + Subject + Base Verb?

"Why did you choose Antarctica?" → Answer: "To study penguins."

"What did she say in her email?" → Answer: "The whale was rescued."

2. Subject Questions (Who / What asks for the subject):

Who / What + Simple Past Verb (-ed or irregular)?

• When Who or What is the subject of the question: DO NOT USE DID!

✓ Who called? (Answer: "My sister.")
✗ Who did call?

✓ Who gave the presentation yesterday? (Answer: "Sheila.")
✗ Who did give the presentation?

Lesson 2 • Charts 2.3 - 2.4

Past Progressive: Actions in Progress & Past Scene Setting

In Progress at Past Time • Scene Descriptions • Non-Action Rule

2 Major Uses of Past Progressive

1. Action in Progress at a Specific Time in the Past:

"They were studying at 9:00 last night."

"At 6:00 p.m., many people in town were listening to Angelica’s radio show."

2. Setting the Scene in a Narrative:

"The café was full. A band was playing, and people were talking loudly. Everyone was enjoying the evening."

"Several students were studying while others were using their phones."

Affirmative: Subject + was / were + Verb-ing
"Angelica was teaching Tzuthil in the evenings."
Negative: Subject + was not (wasn't) / were not (weren't) + Verb-ing
"He wasn't studying; he was reading a magazine."

Non-Action (Stative) Verbs in the Past

Non-Action Verbs Rule:

Non-action verbs describing mental states, emotions, senses, or possession (*know, believe, understand, see, love, own, belong*) are NOT usually used with the past progressive.

✓ She knew how to speak Xyzyl. | ✗ She was knowing how to speak...

✓ I saw an accident last night. | ✗ I was seeing an accident...

✓ Debbie owned a nice car. | ✗ Debbie was owning a nice car.

Past Progressive Questions

Yes/No: "Were they looking for a musician? → No, they weren't."

Wh- Question: "Where were you studying last night? → At the library."

Subject Question: "Who was singing last night? → Alex and Rui." (Verb is 3rd-person singular 'was').

Lesson 3 • Charts 2.5 - 2.6

Past Time Clauses: When, While & Sequence Rules

Interrupted Actions • Events in Sequence • Comma Rule

A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a verb. A main clause can stand alone as a complete sentence, whereas a past time clause begins with a time word (when or while) and tells when the main event took place.

Pattern 1

Action in Progress Interrupted by an Event

Use while / when + past progressive for the action already in progress (longer action), and when + simple past for the interrupting event (shorter action).

While + Progressive: While he was skiing, an avalanche started.
When + Simple Past: Chin was going down the mountain when the mountainside came down.

The skiing started first; the avalanche interrupted it.

Pattern 2

Events in Sequence (One After Another)

When two actions happened one after the other, use the simple past in BOTH the time clause and the main clause. Use when to introduce the first event.

When her phone rang (1st event), she answered it (2nd event).
When the ocean water froze (1st), ice surrounded the Endurance (2nd).

Both verbs are simple past because one action completed before the second began.

Strict Comma Rule for Time Clauses
Time Clause FIRST → Use Comma (,) "When it started to snow, I was walking home."
Main Clause FIRST → NO Comma "I was walking home when it started to snow."
Lesson 4 • Charts 2.7 - 2.9

Repeated Past Actions: Used To and Would

Habits • Past States • "Would" Restrictions

Used To (Habits AND States)

Universal

Expresses a habit, routine, or condition in the past that no longer exists now.

• Past Habit: "I used to run several times a week." (Now I don't).
• Past State: "There used to be a supermarket near my home."
• Negative: "I didn't use to eat meat." (Note spelling: use to, no 'd'!)
Question Forms: "Did you use to live here?" | Subject: "Who used to teach this class?"

Would (Actions ONLY)

Actions Only

Expresses a repeated past action or routine that no longer happens.

• "In high school, we would play soccer every afternoon."
• "They would explore a historic site and bring objects back."
• "My father would take me to the zoo once a month."
CRUCIAL TRAP: "Would" Cannot Express States!

Do NOT use would for past situations or stative verbs (be, live, own, have):

✓ There used to be a library on this street.
✗ There would be a library on this street.

Writing Focus • Connect to Writing

Identifying & Correcting Sentence Fragments

Complete Sentences • Subordinate Clauses

A sentence fragment is an incomplete sentence masquerading as a full one. In academic English, every sentence MUST have an independent subject, a complete finite verb, and express a finished thought.

Missing Subject Fragment

The verb is present, but there is no subject performing the action:

✗ Waited on our roof for help.
→ Add Subject: "My family and I waited on our roof for help."

Missing Verb Fragment

The predicate verb is omitted or replaced by a hanging phrase:

✗ Everything in its path.
→ Add Verb: "The flood was covering everything in its path."

Isolated Time Clause Fragment

A dependent time clause punctuated as an independent sentence:

✗ While the water was rising.
→ Connect Main Clause: "While the water was rising, we climbed onto the roof."
Narrative Writing Proofreading Checklist:
Used simple past for completed narrative actions.
Used past progressive to set the background scene.
Used commas after introductory past time clauses.
Verified that every sentence contains both a subject and a verb.
