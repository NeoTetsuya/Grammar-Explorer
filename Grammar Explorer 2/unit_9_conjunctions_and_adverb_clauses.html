1. Conjunctions (And, Or, So, But)

The Four Coordinators

  • And (Addition) Connects similar ideas.
    "She likes to swim and play the drums."
  • Or (Option / Choice) Presents alternative possibilities.
    "Do you want pie or fruit for dessert?"
  • So (Result) Introduces a result. Connects independent clauses (never words).
    "It was raining hard, so I took the bus."
  • But (Contrast) Shows contrast or difference between ideas.
    "She lived in Mexico, but she never learned Spanish."

Punctuation Rules

No Comma: Connecting Two Words / Phrases

Do not place a comma when the conjunction connects only two words or short phrases.

Tennis is fun but difficult. (No comma before "but")
Use Comma: Connecting Two Independent Clauses

Always place a comma before the conjunction when connecting two complete sentences.

Do you want to go out to eat, or do you want to stay home?

Expressing Similarity: Too, So, Either, Neither

Affirmative Statements

  • Subject + Auxiliary + TOO Linda likes to swim, and Luc does too.
  • SO + Auxiliary + Subject Linda likes to swim, and so does Luc.

Negative Statements

  • Subject + Aux(Neg) + EITHER Mae doesn’t eat fish, and Kim doesn’t either.
  • NEITHER + Aux(Affirmative) + Subject Mae doesn’t eat fish, and neither does Kim.

Real English Agreement in Conversation

Agreeing with Affirmative A: "I love San Francisco."
B: "So do I."
Agreeing with Negative A: "Tina has never been to Europe."
B: "Neither have I."

2. Adverb Clauses: Cause & Contrast

Cause (Because, Since)

State the reason for an outcome.

"Since it was raining hard, we stayed home."
"We stayed home because it was raining hard."

Contrast (Although, Even though)

Introduce an unexpected or surprising difference.

"Although golf is popular, I don't like it."
"We took a walk even though it was raining."

3. Future Conditionals & If vs. When

Tense Rule for Future Conditionals

If-Clause (Condition) MUST use Simple Present.
Never use will / be going to inside 'if'!
Main Clause (Result) Uses Will or Be going to.
Example: "If a dolphin finds food, it will call other dolphins."

IF = Uncertain

Describes a future situation that is possible, but not certain (it may or may not happen).

"If we see a bear, I'll be scared." (We might not see a bear).

WHEN = Certain

Describes a future situation that is certain to happen.

"When they go hiking tomorrow, they'll take pictures." (They are definitely going hiking).

Writing Focus: Correcting Comma Splices

A comma splice happens when two independent sentences are glued together with only a comma. Fix it by adding a coordinating conjunction (and, but, so, or) after the comma!

Noise pollution is all around us, it sometimes causes serious problems.
Noise pollution is all around us, and it sometimes causes serious problems.
