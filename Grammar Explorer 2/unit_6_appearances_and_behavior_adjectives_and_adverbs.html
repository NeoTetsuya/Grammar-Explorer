1. Adjectives & Nouns as Adjectives

Placement, Form Rules & Modifying Nouns

Adjective Placement

• Before Nouns: They have thick fur.
• After Linking Verbs: The fox looks sleek. (Describes the subject).

Form Constraints & Articles

• No Plural Form: Adjectives NEVER take -s! (ancients towns → ancient towns).
• A vs. An: Based on initial sound! (a good university, an old apartment).

Nouns as Adjectives

• Place a noun directly before another noun to describe it: a park bench, a school nurse.
Must Remain Singular:
planes tickets → plane tickets (even if the main noun is plural!).

Punctuation Rules for Adjectives

• Same Type of Info: Use commas or and (a kind, patient person OR kind and patient).
• Different Types of Info: NO commas! (a big orange cat, short curly blonde hair).
Writers rarely use more than three adjectives together before a single noun.

3. Adverbs of Manner

Describing Actions, Forms & Placement

Usage & Placement

• Describe how an action verb is performed (He spoke softly. She dances beautifully.).
Placement Rule: Place after the verb or verb + object. NEVER place an adverb between a verb and its direct object!

Forms & Irregulars

• Standard Formation: Add -ly to adjective (soft → softly).
• Identical Forms: early, fast, hard, late are both adjectives AND adverbs.
• Irregular Form: good (adj) → well (adv). (My laptop works well.)
-ly Adjectives Trap: friendly, lonely, ugly, lovely are ADJECTIVES, not adverbs!

4. Adverbs of Degree & Writing Focus

Modifying Intensity & Descriptive Writing Guidelines

Adverbs of Degree (Directly before Adj/Adv)
• Stronger: extremely, so, really, very, quite, pretty.
• Weaker: somewhat, fairly.
Writing Focus: Animal & Character Descriptions

Use descriptive adjectives and adverbs to make details vivid, but avoid overusing them in academic or business writing.

"Monkey climbing with arms" → "Climbing with long arms and a powerful tail."
