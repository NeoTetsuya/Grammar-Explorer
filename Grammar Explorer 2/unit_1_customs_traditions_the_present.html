Lesson 1 • Chart 1.1 - 1.2

Simple Present: Statements, Forms & Questions

Habits • Facts • Repeated Events

When to Use Simple Present

  • Habits & Repeated Actions: "We eat dinner at 7:30." / "Jim visits his uncle twice a year."
  • Facts & General Truths: "The mail doesn’t come on Sundays." / "It snows a lot in Finland."
  • Traditions & Cultural Customs: "In Afar culture, the father represents the bride at the ceremony."
Type Subject Verb / Auxiliary Example
Affirmative I / You / We / They eat / wear "They eat a traditional meal."
Affirmative He / She / It eats / teaches "Rosa teaches cultural anthropology."
Negative I / You / We / They do not / don't + base "They don’t live together yet."
Negative He / She / It does not / doesn't + base "The bride doesn’t attend the male ceremony."
Be careful: In negative sentences, use the base verb form after doesn't.
✗ Rosa doesn't teaches math. → ✓ Rosa doesn't teach math.

Questions & Subject Questions

Yes / No Questions & Short Answers

Do / Does + Subject + Base Form?

"Do you speak Russian?"

→ Yes, I do. / No, I don’t.

"Does he sing?"

→ Yes, he does. / No, he doesn’t.

Crucial Difference Object Questions vs. Subject Questions
1. Object Wh- Questions (asking about the object/place/time):

Wh- Word + do / does + Subject + Base Verb?

"Where does Jeff live?" → Answer: "In Sydney."

"What do people give the newlyweds?" → Answer: "Gifts."

2. Subject Questions (Who / What asks for the subject):

Who / What + Verb (-s / -es) + Object?

• When Who or What is the subject, the verb takes 3rd person singular form (-s/-es).

• NEVER USE do or does!

✓ Who teaches this class? (Answer: "Professor Ortega.")
✗ Who does teach this class?

✓ Who pays for the Afar wedding? (Answer: "The man.")
✗ Who does pay for the wedding?

Frequency Adverbs & Word Order Rules

Scale • Placement • Negative Inversion

Frequency Scale Spectrum (0% to 100%)

100% always
~90% usually
~70% often / frequently
~50% sometimes
~10% rarely / seldom / hardly ever
0% never
Rule 1
After the Verb "BE"

Always place the frequency adverb immediately after am, is, are.

I am sometimes early for class.

Jenny isn’t often late.

Wedding guests are usually happy.

✗ Jenny often isn't late.

Rule 2
Before Main Verbs

Place the frequency adverb immediately before normal action verbs.

I usually enjoy parties.

Marco hardly ever arrives on time.

We don’t always remember birthdays.

✗ I enjoy usually parties.

Rule 3
Flexible Positions & "Ever"

Sometimes, usually, frequently, and often can also begin or end a sentence.

Usually Western brides wear white.

Brides wear red dresses sometimes.

Do you ever eat at restaurants?

Ever means "at any time" in questions.

Double Negative Warning with Rarely / Seldom / Never

Adverbs like never, rarely, seldom, and hardly ever already carry negative meaning. Do not use them with negative verbs (don't / doesn't).

✓ They rarely eat fast food.  |  ✗ They don't rarely eat fast food.

Lesson 2 • Chart 1.4 - 1.5

Present Progressive: Actions in Progress & Temporary Trends

Now • Around Now • Temporary Situations

2 Major Uses of Present Progressive

1. Actions Happening Right Now (at the moment of speaking):

"Look! They are dancing in the street."

"The women are lighting sparklers for Diwali right now."

2. Actions in Progress Around Now (not necessarily right this second):

"My class is studying world history this semester."

"Where are you living these days? I am renting an apartment."

"Thousands of people are attending the festival this year."

Key Signal Words for Progressive:
now right now at the moment this year this semester these days Look! / Listen!

Sentence Formulas & Questions

Affirmative: Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing
"She is studying in England this year."
Negative: Subject + am/is/are + not + Verb-ing
"I am not preparing the food for the party."
Yes/No Question: Am/Is/Are + Subject + Verb-ing?
"Is your phone ringing? → Yes, it is."
Subject Questions with Who / What

When Who or What is the subject, always use the 3rd-person singular form: is + Verb-ing.

Who + is + Verb-ing...?

"Who is dancing with Carmen?" → Answer: "Her father."

"What is happening over there?" → Answer: "They are racing carts."

Lesson 2 • Chart 1.6

Action vs. Non-Action (Stative) Verbs

Feelings • Senses • Possession • Dual Meaning

Action verbs describe physical or mental activities (run, study, eat, celebrate) and can be used in both simple and progressive forms.

Non-action (stative) verbs describe states, conditions, sensations, or feelings. They are NOT usually used in the progressive (-ing), even if happening right now!

Feelings

dislike, hate, like, love, miss

"I love my city." (NOT: I am loving)

Senses

feel, hear, see, smell, sound, taste

"The soup smells delicious."

Possession

belong, have, own

"He doesn't own a camera."

Appearance

appear, look, seem

"Ted looks tired today."

Desires

hope, need, prefer, want

"They want a happy future."

Mental States

believe, think, understand, know

"She understands Japanese."

Writing Focus • Connect to Writing

Capitalizing Proper Nouns

Places • Dates • People • Festivals

Proper nouns name specific individual people, places, events, and calendar periods. In English, proper nouns must always begin with a capital letter.

Geographical Names

Streets, cities, countries, continents, and named geographic regions:

• Tucker Street
• Paris, New York City, Buñol
• Djibouti, Kosovo, India, Spain
• Asia, Africa’s Great Rift Valley
• the West Coast (region vs. "drive west")

Days, Months & Holidays

Days of the week, months, and official festival or holiday celebrations:

• Monday, Friday, krada
• January, February, August
• Diwali, Independence Day, Carnival
• Thanksgiving Day, 4th of July
Note: Seasons (spring, summer, fall, winter) are usually lowercase unless part of a title!

People, Groups & Festivals

Specific people, formal titles, deities, cultural groups, and event names:

• Rosa, Jeff, Professor Ortega, Marta
• Lakshmi (Hindu deity)
• Asante, Afar, Maori (peoples)
• La Tomatina
• Kila Raipur Sports Festival
Quick Proofreading Check:
✗ They go to tucker street. → ✓ Tucker Street
✗ We celebrate diwali in october. → ✓ Diwali in October
✗ Rosa teach math at university. → ✓ Rosa teaches math...
✗ In the afar culture... → ✓ In the Afar culture...
