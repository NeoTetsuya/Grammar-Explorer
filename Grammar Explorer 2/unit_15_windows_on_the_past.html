1. Active vs. Passive Voice & Tense Forms

Active vs. Passive Role

  • Active Voice: Subject is the agent / doer performing the action.
    "Archaeologists discovered the artifact."
  • Passive Voice (be + Past Participle): Subject is the receiver of the action.
    "The artifact was discovered."
  • Avoiding Blame Technique Useful when you want to describe an error or issue without pointing fingers:
    "The procedure wasn't followed." (vs. "You made a mistake.")

Transitive Verb Constraint

Transitive Verbs ONLY! Only verbs taking a direct object in the active voice can become passive!
Intransitive Verbs (NO passive form): arrive, sleep, come, fall, happen, disappear.
"Our taxi arrived early." "Our taxi was arrived early."

Passive Forms Across Tenses

Present Passive is / are (not) + Past Participle • "These cars aren't made in Germany." • Question: "Is this car made in Korea?"
Past Passive was / were (not) + Past Participle • "The meeting was canceled." • Question: "Where were the cars made?"
Future Passive will / won't be + Past Participle • "The plans won't be changed." • Question: "Where will the food be kept?"

2. Passive Voice with Modals & The "By" Phrase

Modal Passives

Formula: Subject + Modal (not) + be + Past Participle

  • Present / General: "The topics can be researched online."
  • Future Possibility: "The report might not be finished on time."
  • Past Ability: "The ship couldn't be seen in the storm."
  • Questions with Modals: Place modal before subject: "When should the project be finished?"

When to Use or Omit "By + Agent"

INCLUDE the "By" Phrase when: It states vital, specific new information or a famous creator/number:
• "The sunken ship was found by some divers."
OMIT the "By" Phrase when: The agent is unknown or clear from context (e.g., government, police, people):
• "The items were stolen late last night." (Unknown agent)
• "New laws were passed yesterday." (Obvious: by government)
Agent Questions In passive questions asking about the agent, place by at the very end:
"Who was it written by?"

3. Participial Adjectives: Feelings (-ed) vs. Characteristics (-ing)

Past Participial Adjectives (-ed)

Describes a person's FEELINGS or mental states (passive effect on the person).

• "People are fascinated by the news."
• "I was confused by the instructions."
*Can be regular (-ed) or irregular (e.g. broken, forgotten).

Present Participial Adjectives (-ing)

Describes the CHARACTERISTICS or cause of feelings (a person, place, or thing).

• "This is a fascinating movie."
• "Her trip to China sounded exciting."
*Placed before nouns or after linking verbs (be, sound, look).

The Feelings (-ed) vs. Characteristics (-ing) Matrix

Human Feeling (-ed) "The history students are interested."
(They experience interest in their minds)
Thing/Person Characteristic (-ing) "The history teacher is interesting."
(The teacher creates interest in others)

Writing Focus: Strong Concluding Sentences

When writing a historical narrative or memory essay, always end each paragraph with a strong, definitive concluding sentence to signal paragraph completion.

State an Opinion Conclude by reflecting on personal perspective.
• "It seems he was rarely without his pen."
Provide a Clear Summary Summarize main points or importance.
• "It is one of the most important memories of my life."
