Pronunciation of -s / -es Endings

1. /s/: After voiceless consonants /f/, /k/, /p/, /t/, /θ/ → beliefs, books, cups, tests, months.

2. /z/: After voiced consonants /b/, /d/, /g/, /l/, /m/, /n/, /r/, /v/ or vowels → beds, dogs, calls, songs, wives, days.

3. /əz/ or /ɪz/: After sibilants /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/, /ks/ → classes, dishes, watches, judge's, boxes, diseases.

Possessive Nouns & The Matrix of Another / Other

Ownership • Joint vs Separate • Quantifying Determiners

Formation of Possessive Nouns

Noun Type Formation Rule Textbook Example
Singular Noun noun + 's "A sangoma's goal is to heal." / "The child's mother."
Regular Plural (ends in -s) plural noun + ' "The girls' bicycles." / "The doctors' white coats."
Irregular Plural (no -s) plural noun + 's "The children's section." / "The women's swim team."
Joint Possession (Shared) 's on 2nd noun only "John and Tina's father is a doctor." (One shared father)
Separate Possession 's on BOTH nouns "John's and Tina's test results are ready." (Two distinct tests)
Common Apostrophe Mistakes

✗ Doctor Chen has a lot of patient's. → ✓ Doctor Chen has a lot of patients. (Simple plural, no ownership!)

✗ Do you know your bodies limits? → ✓ Do you know your body's limits?

Another vs. Other vs. The Other Matrix

Indefinite Singular another + singular count

One more of the same, or an alternative person/thing.

"I need another glass of water."

Definite Singular the other + singular count

The second of two specific persons/things.

"One knee hurts; the other knee is fine."

Indefinite Plural other + plural count

Some, but not all, remaining persons/things.

"Other patients prefer traditional healing."

Definite Plural the other + plural count

All the rest of a specific known group.

"Two nurses are new; the other six are experienced."

Pronoun Usage (One / Ones & Others)

• Replacement: "Lydia's watch broke, so she bought another one." | "These shoes are worn out; I prefer the other ones."

• Standalone Pronouns: "Some runners sprinted; others jogged slowly." (Never say other people jogged slowly when using the pronoun others!).

Count vs. Non-Count Nouns: 4 Core Categories & Dual Meanings

Grammar Rules • Superfoods • Meaning Shifts

3 Foundational Grammar Rules for Non-Count Nouns

1. No Plural -s: Non-count nouns name whole masses or concepts without separate items.
✗ She gave me many informations. → ✓ She gave me a lot of information.

2. Never Use A / An: Do not place a or an directly before a non-count noun.
✗ I need an advice. → ✓ I need some advice. / I need a piece of advice.

3. Always Use Singular Verbs & Pronouns:
✓ Rice is cheap, but I don’t like it. (✗ Rice are cheap, I don't like them).

The 4 Non-Count Classification Categories

1. Category Aggregates clothing, furniture, homework, jewelry, luggage, mail, money, traffic, weather, equipment, trash.
2. Solids, Liquids & Gases air, cheese, coffee, flour, hair, ice, juice, meat, milk, oil, rice, skin, soup, sugar, tea, water.
3. Abstract Concepts advice, energy, experience, fun, health, help, honesty, intelligence, knowledge, progress, stress, work.
4. Fields of Study & Sports biology, chemistry, geometry, history, math, physics, science, yoga, athletics.

Dual-Meaning Nouns: Contextual Meaning Shifts

Several common nouns in health and fitness change from non-count (broad mass/concept) to count (specific instance, event, or portion):

Noun Non-Count Meaning (Mass / General) Count Meaning (Specific Instance / Unit)
exercise General physical activity:
"Exercise is important for health."		 Specific workout routine/task:
"We had three exercises for homework."
time Duration / clock time:
"I don’t have time to go to the gym."		 Occasions / repeated events:
"She swims four times a week."
experience Knowledge accumulated over time:
"He has ten years of clinical experience."		 A specific life event or trip:
"Hiking the Andes was a memorable experience."
hair Entire head of hair:
"Argan oil keeps your hair soft."		 Individual strands:
"I found two dog hairs on my workout jacket."
paper Material in bulk:
"This cup is made of paper."		 An academic essay or daily newspaper:
"She wrote a scientific paper on nutrition."

Quantity Words & Partitive Measurement Structures

Quantifiers • Positive vs Negative Nuance • Containers & Units

Quantifier Distribution Rules

Quantifier Used With Sentence Types & Example
some Count (pl.) & Non-Count Affirmative: "We need some bananas and some water."
any Count (pl.) & Non-Count Negatives & Questions: "We don't have any milk or any apples."
many / a few Count Nouns ONLY "Many athletes entered." / "I have a few questions."
much / a little Non-Count Nouns ONLY "Don't drink much soda." / "Add a little olive oil."
a lot of / lots of Both Count & Non-Count Informal & Common: "a lot of vegetables / a lot of sugar"
Advanced Nuance: "A Few / A Little" vs. "Few / Little"
Positive Nuance (Some / Sufficient): • a few: "He has a few friends at the gym." (He is content).
• a little: "There is a little water left." (Enough to drink).
Negative Nuance (Almost None / Lacking): • few: "He has few friends at the gym." (He feels lonely).
• little: "There is little water left." (We are dangerously thirsty).

Writing Focus • Connect to Writing

Subject-Verb Agreement with Count and Non-Count Nouns

Academic Writing • Concord • Proofreading Checklist

In formal and academic writing, verbs must strictly agree in number with their subjects. Errors frequently occur when writers confuse non-count mass nouns with plurals or get distracted by prepositional phrases.

Non-Count Subjects

Always take third-person singular verb forms (-s/-es, is, was, has):

✓ Exercise is essential.
✓ Fresh fruit tastes great.
✗ Exercise are essential.

Plural Count Subjects

Take plural verb forms (base form, are, were, have):

✓ Many athletes get hurt.
✓ These shoes are comfortable.
✗ Many athletes gets hurt.

Quantifiers with "Of"

With most of, all of, half of, 50% of, the verb agrees with the noun after of:

• Most of the water is clean.
• Most of the players are ready.
Quantities of Distance, Time, Money, Weight

When expressing a unified total quantity, use a singular verb:

"Five kilometers is a long run."

"Thirty minutes of walking was enough."

"One of the" Construction

The true grammatical subject is one, requiring a singular verb:

"One of the players is injured." (NOT: are)

"One of my favorite foods is salmon."

Health & Fitness Opinion Writing Checklist:
Used a or an before singular count nouns (a walk, an apple).
Avoided a / an and plural -s with non-count nouns (exercise, clothing).
Used some in affirmative and any in negative statements.
Checked that non-count subjects have singular verbs (The food isn't good).
