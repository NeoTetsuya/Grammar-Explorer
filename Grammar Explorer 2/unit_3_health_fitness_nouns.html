1. Plurals, Possessives & Determiners

Irregulars, Foreign Plurals, Joint Ownership & Substitutions

A. Plural Spelling & Foreign Roots

• Regular Rules: Add -s (athletes), -es after s/sh/ch/x (dishes, classes), -ies after consonant+y (facilities).
• -f / -fe Endings: leaf → leaves, knife → knives (Exceptions: roofs, beliefs, chiefs).
• Foreign & Scientific Plurals (Latin/Greek):
Scientific analyses show clear results.
• analysis → analyses | diagnosis → diagnoses | thesis → theses
• bacterium → bacteria | criterion → criteria | datum → data | stimulus → stimuli
• Compound Nouns: Modify head noun: mothers-in-law, passers-by, runners-up (BUT fitness centers, spoonfuls).
• Pair Nouns (Plural-Only): scissors, shorts, leggings, tights, glasses → Take plural verb, or use a pair of shorts is.
• Singular -s Nouns: diabetes, news, gymnastics, athletics, physics → Take singular verbs.

B. Possessive Nouns & Special Forms

• Singular Noun: the child's room ('s).
• Plural (-s): the girls' bicycles (apostrophe after -s). Irregular plural: the children's room.
• Joint Possession (Shared): "John and Tina's father" (One father for both → 's on 2nd noun only).
• Separate Possession (Distinct): "John's and Tina's trainers" (Two different pairs of trainers → 's on BOTH nouns).
• Time & Distance Possessives: "a minute's rest", "thirty minutes' cardio", "a mile's walk".

Another vs. Other vs. Others Matrix

Another + Singular Count:
"another workout / piece" (indefinite: one more)
The other + Singular Count:
"the other doctor" (definite: 2nd of two)
Other + Plural / Non-Count:
"other athletes / advice" (indefinite)
The other + Plural / Non-Count:
"the other shoes / equipment" (definite remaining)
• Standalone Pronouns: others ("Some exercise; others rest."), the others ("Three finished; the others failed.").
• Idioms: every other day (alternate days), one after another.

2. Count vs. Non-Count Nouns

6 Non-Count Categories & Dual Meaning Shifts

Core Distinction

• Count Nouns: Countable units (an apple / three apples). Singular requires a/an/the/my.
• Non-Count Nouns: Uncountable mass/concepts. NO plural -s! NO a/an! ALWAYS takes singular verbs.

The 6 Non-Count Categories

1. Aggregates / Collections: clothing, equipment, furniture, luggage, machinery, trash, advice, news.
2. Liquids & Nutrients: water, blood, oxygen, protein, fat, iron, calcium, rice, sugar.
3. Abstract Concepts: health, fitness, progress, research, knowledge, evidence, stress, patience.
4. Fields of Study & Sports: cardiology, nutrition, biology, gymnastics, swimming, yoga.
5. Natural Phenomena: weather, heat, sunlight, climate, lightning.
6. Languages & General: English, French, vocabulary.

Dual-Meaning Nouns (Count vs. Non-Count Shift)

• exercise: [U] General activity ("Exercise is essential") vs. [C] Specific routine ("Do 3 exercises").
• time: [U] Duration ("I don't have time") vs. [C] Occasions ("I worked out 4 times").
• experience: [U] Knowledge over time ("10 years of experience") vs. [C] Event ("a great experience").
• hair: [U] Head of hair ("She has dark hair") vs. [C] Individual strand ("two hairs on the sofa").
• paper: [U] Material ("made of paper") vs. [C] Academic essay/newspaper ("publish two papers").
• work: [U] Labor/job ("a lot of work") vs. [C] Artistic/literary creations ("works of art").

3. Quantifiers & Partitive Measurement

Quantifiers Matrix, Few vs. A Few & Unit Partitioners

A. Quantifiers Distribution Matrix

Count Only: a few, few, many, several, a number of, both, each, every
Non-Count Only: a little, little, much, a great deal of, a large amount of
Both Count & Non-Count: some, any, a lot of, lots of, plenty of, enough, most, all, no
Nuance Alert:
• a few / a little = Positive nuance (some, enough) → "He has a few friends at the gym."
• few / little = Negative nuance (almost none, insufficient) → "He has few friends and feels lonely."

B. Partitive Structures (Measurement Word + Of + Non-Count)

To count non-count items, attach a partitive container, portion, unit, or abstract counter:

• Containers: a bottle of water, a carton of milk, a tube of ointment, a jar of honey, a can of tuna
• Portions / Shapes: a slice of bread, a bar of soap, a stick of butter, a scoop of protein powder, a clove of garlic
• Abstract Counters: a piece of advice, an item of news, a stroke of luck, a fit of coughing, a bit of help
• Standard Units: a gram of protein, a liter of water, a pinch of salt, a tablespoon of oil, a dose of medicine

Writing Focus

8 Essential Subject-Verb Agreement Rules

1. Non-Count Nouns → Singular Verb • Exercise is essential. | Fresh fruit tastes delicious.
2. Plural Count Nouns → Plural Verb • Many athletes get injured. | Three slices are enough.
3. Singular Meaning in -s → Singular Verb • Diabetes is manageable. | The news was encouraging.
4. Quantifiers with "Of" (half of, 80% of, most of, all of) • Agrees with the noun after of: "Most of the water is clean" vs. "Most of the runners are ready".
5. "One of" + Plural Noun → Singular Verb • One of the players has won the marathon.
6. Quantities of Distance, Time, Weight, Money → Singular Verb • Five kilometers is a long run. | 100 grams of protein is required daily.
Pro-Tip for Academic Writing

Never add an '-s' to equipment, advice, information, research, furniture. To make them plural in counting, use partitives like "three pieces of equipment" or "items of advice"!
