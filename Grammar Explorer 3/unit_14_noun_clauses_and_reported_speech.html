Noun Clauses & Reported Speech

Learn how to embed ideas inside other sentences using Noun Clauses, and master the art of shifting tenses to accurately report what someone else said.

1. Noun Clauses

A noun clause is a dependent clause that acts like a noun. It contains its own subject and verb.

Introduced by "That"

Follows verbs of mental activity (believe, think) or adjectives describing feelings (glad, sure).

I discovered (that) the fridge was broken.
The problem is that we have no food.

Introduced by "If/Whether"

Turns a Yes/No question into a noun clause. Must use statement word order!

Do you know whether he is coming?
She asked if I wanted dessert.

Introduced by "Wh-" Words

Turns information questions into noun clauses. Do not use do/does/did!

I'm not sure where the park is.
Can I ask where they live?
The "Or Not" Rule The phrase or not can go directly after whether, or at the end of the clause. However, it cannot go directly after if.
I don't know whether or not I passed. I don't know if or not I passed.
Prepositions Rule Always use whether (not if) after a verb + preposition combination.
She talked about whether to move.

2. Reported Speech: The Backward Shift

When the reporting verb is in the past (e.g., said, told), the verb tense and certain modals in the reported clause shift backward in time.

Verb Tense Shifts

  • Simple Present Simple Past
  • Present Prog. Past Prog.
  • Simple Past Past Perfect
  • Present Perfect Past Perfect
  • am/is/are going to was/were going to

Modal Shifts

  • will would
  • can could
  • must had to
  • may might
No Change Required For: should, ought to, could, might.
General Truth Exception: If the reported statement describes a general truth or something still true today, the verb tense does not need to change.
"I told him that I love to cook." (I still love to cook).

3. Reporting Questions, Commands & Time

Reported Questions

Reported questions use statement word order. Tenses backshift according to the standard rules.

Wh- Questions: "How long has she talked?" →
Omar asked how long she had talked.
Yes/No Questions: "Did he take photos?" →
Carol asked if he had taken photos.

Commands & Advice

Use an infinitive phrase (to + base verb) to report commands or advice. Do not use the word "please".

Affirmative: "Turn off your phones." →
The teacher told us to turn off our phones.
Negative: "Don't cut the cake." →
Joe told me not to cut the cake.

Time and Place Shifts

now → then
today → that day
tomorrow → the next day
yesterday → the day before
next week → the following week
last year → the year before
here → there
this → that

Writing Connection: Using "According To"

To report someone's opinion or survey results in writing, use the phrase according to + name/pronoun.

Punctuation Rule: Always place a comma after the phrase when it introduces an opinion at the start of a sentence.
"According to her, no one else makes such a delicious salad."
