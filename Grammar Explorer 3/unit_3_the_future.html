Will vs. Be Going To

Predictions, Decisions, and Plans.

Will (Spontaneous & Offers)

Use for sudden decisions made at the moment of speaking, promises, and requests.

"The phone is ringing!" → "I will answer it."

Be Going To (Plans & Evidence)

Use for actions already decided upon (intentions) or predictions based on existing present evidence.

Look at those clouds. It is going to rain.
Next year, I am going to study in Japan.
Note: Both can be used for general predictions (e.g., "Robots will/are going to take over jobs").

Present Tenses for the Future

Schedules and Definite Arrangements.

Simple Present (Schedules)

Used for future events that are part of a fixed timetable or schedule (flights, meetings, store hours).

The train leaves at 8:00 AM tomorrow.

Present Progressive (Definite Plans)

Used for definite plans, usually in the near future, where time/place details are known.

I am meeting Sarah for lunch at noon.
Verb Constraint: With the verbs go and come, the present progressive is preferred over be going to.
Prefer "We are going to the movies" over "We are going to go..."

Future Time Clauses: The Golden Rule

An adverbial future time clause tells when a main action will happen. Never use a future form (will/going to) inside the time clause itself!

Incorrect Structure

As soon as he will arrive, we will eat.

Correct Structure

As soon as he arrives, we will eat.
Time Clause First Use after, when, as soon as, once. Comma required!
"Once I finish, I'll call you."
Boundary (Until) Action continues up to the time clause.
"I will wait until she calls."
Completion Emphasis Use Present Perfect in the clause!
"After I have finished, I will leave."

Progressive & Perfect Futures

Future Progressive

will be + verb-ing

Actions actively in progress at a specific time in the future.

This time tomorrow, I will be flying to London.

Future Perfect

will have + past participle

An action fully completed before a specific future time.

By 2050, we will have built a base on Mars.

Future Perfect Prog.

will have been + verb-ing

Duration of an ongoing action up until a future time.

By December, I will have been working here for 5 years.

Writing Connection: Opinions & Fragments

When writing about the future:

  • Use believe and think to express structured opinions.
  • Use hope to express a future wish or desire.
Fragment Warning: An if clause must always be attached to a main clause.
If robots take our jobs. We will need basic income.
If robots take our jobs, we will need basic income.
