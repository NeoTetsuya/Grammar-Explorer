Present Perfect

Connecting Past to Present

Use have/has + past participle for actions starting in the past continuing to the present, or completed actions relevant now.

For (Amount of time) I have lived here for ten years.
Since (Specific start point) She has taught math since 2018.

Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

The choice depends on whether the exact time is known/completed or unspecific/ongoing.

We have visited Rome twice.
Exact time NOT given / Experience
We visited Rome in 2019.
Exact past time explicitly given

Adverb Placement

  • Mid-sentence: already, always, ever, never, just go between the auxiliary and the past participle.
    "I have already eaten."
  • End of sentence: yet, so far, lately, recently typically appear at the end.
    "He hasn't arrived yet."
Style Note: When two present perfect verbs are connected by "and", do not repeat the auxiliary verb.
Example: He has traveled to Africa and has taken amazing photos.

Present Perfect Progressive

Structure: have/has + been + verb-ing. Used for ongoing, continuous actions starting in the past and continuing into the present.

Completed vs. Continuous

  • Completed (Result) I have read that book.
  • Continuous (Process) I have been reading that book all week.

How Many vs. How Long

  • How Many/Much (Present Perfect) She has taken 50 photos today.
  • How Long (Present Perfect Progressive) She has been taking photos for hours.

Visible Present Evidence & Temporary Actions

You can use this tense without "for" or "since" to emphasize that an action is temporary, happened very recently, or has a visible result in the present.

"The ground is wet." "It has been raining."

Stative Verbs Warning

Progressive forms cannot be used with stative (non-action) verbs like know, like, own.

I have been knowing him.
I have known him for years.

Routine Verbs

For verbs expressing routine (live, work, teach, study), both tenses have very similar meanings.

"I have worked here" ≈ "I have been working here"

The Past Perfect (The Past before the Past)

Structure: had + past participle. Used to indicate that one past action occurred before another past action or specific time.

Past Perfect (Completed)

One completed action happened before another.

By the time I arrived, the train had departed.

Past Perfect Progressive (Ongoing)

An ongoing action was in progress up to a point in the past.

She had been waiting for an hour when he finally arrived.

Time Clauses with "By"

"By" means before or not later than. Paired with past perfect.

  • By + time: By 2010, he had climbed three mountains.
  • By the time + simple past: By the time she woke up, the storm had passed.

Unreal Conditionals

When referring to an unreal or hypothetical past, the past perfect is used in the if clause.

If she had studied more, she would have passed.

Writing Connection: Sequencing Events

When writing a personal narrative, use First and After that to introduce the order of events.

Punctuation Rule: Always place a comma directly after these sequence words when they start a sentence.

"First, I gathered my gear. After that, I started the climb."
