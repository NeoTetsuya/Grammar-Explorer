1. Rules of the Road: Necessity & Prohibition

Necessity (Required)

  • Must: Strict rules, laws, and commands. (Rarely used in questions).
    You must show your passport.
  • Have to: General necessity. Can change tense (had to, will have to, has had to).
    She has to pack her bags.
  • Have got to: Informal necessity. Affirmative statements only.
    We have got to leave now!

Prohibition vs. Lack of Necessity

Must Not (Prohibition)

You are NOT allowed to do this. It is a strict rule against the action.

You must not smoke on the plane.
Don't Have To (Lack of Necessity)

You have a choice. You can do it, but it is not required.

You don't have to tip the waiter.

Past Obligation Constraint

Never use must have to express a past necessity. You must use had to.

Yesterday, I had to renew my visa.

Expectations (Be Supposed To)

Shows an obligation based on cultural rules or expectations. Use was/were supposed to for unfulfilled past expectations.

I was supposed to meet him, but he didn't show up.

2. The Power to Do: Ability

Present

Can (frequent) or be able to (formal).

I can speak Spanish.
She is able to assist you.

Past

Could (general ability) or was/were able to.

I could run fast as a kid.
We were able to find the hotel.

Future

Use will be able to for a *new* ability. Use can if it's already possible.

I will be able to speak it soon.
I will can speak it.

Advanced "Be Able To" Formats

  • Gerund I enjoy being able to travel freely.
  • Infinitive I want to be able to fly a plane.
  • With Modals You should be able to see the Eiffel Tower from here.

3. If I Were You: Advice & Regrets

General Advice

Use should (common) or ought to. Use should to ask questions.

You should book the tickets early.
Should we pack jackets?

Strong Advice / Warnings

Use had better. Implies negative consequences if ignored. (Refers to Present/Future, NOT Past).

We had better leave now, or we'll miss the flight.
You had better not lose your passport.

Past Regret & Criticism

Structure: Should (not) have + Past Participle. Used to express personal regret or criticize someone's past actions.

Regret (Self) "I should have studied the map. Now we're lost."
Criticism (Others) "You shouldn't have trusted that taxi driver."

Short Answer Format: "Did you buy insurance?" → "No, but I should have." (Drop the past participle).

Writing Connection: Informative Texts & Bullets

When writing an informative guide (like a travel brochure):

  • Use be supposed to for cultural norms, don't have to for optional things, and should for helpful tips.
  • Bullet Points: Introduce points with a bolded word and a colon. Ensure parallel grammar across all bullets.
Tipping Customs in Japan:
  • Restaurants: You don't have to tip the waiter.
  • Taxis: You shouldn't tip the driver; it can cause confusion.
