The Present Tenses

Simple Present

Used for routines, habits, facts, and permanent situations. Adverbs of frequency (usually, always) go before the main verb.

She always wakes up early.

The Earth revolves around the sun.

Present Progressive

Used for actions happening right now, temporary situations, and changes happening over time.

Families are becoming smaller.

He is always leaving his socks on the floor!

Special Rule: Using 'always' with Progressive expresses a complaint!

Crucial Structural Rules

1. Subject Questions (Who / What)

Do not use do/does when Who or What is the subject of the sentence. Conjugate the main verb directly.

Who does want coffee?
Who wants coffee?
2. Omission of 'Be'

When the same subject performs two concurrent actions, do not repeat the auxiliary verb be.

He is reading and is eating.
He is reading and eating.

Stative (Non-Action) Verbs

Verbs that express states or conditions are not typically used in progressive (-ing) forms.

Categories of Stative Verbs

  • Senses: feel, hear, see, smell, taste
  • Mental States: know, think, understand, believe
  • Feelings/Desires: love, hate, want, prefer
  • Possession: have, belong, own
  • Description: appear, seem, cost, weigh
I am wanting a coffee.
I want a coffee.

Dual Meanings (Stative & Active)

Some verbs change meaning depending on the tense used.

Stative (Possession / Belief)
I have a sister.
I think she is nice.
Active (Experiencing / Mental Process)
I am having lunch right now.
I am thinking about my future.

Physical Conditions

Verbs like feel, hurt, and ache can be used in either the simple or progressive form with no change in meaning.

"My head aches."
is the same as
"My head is aching."

Being + Adjective

While 'be' is typically a stative verb, you can use it in the progressive form (is being) to describe a temporary change in someone's personality or behavior.

"He is being very silly today."
(He is acting silly right now, but isn't usually).

Past Tenses & Time Clauses

Simple Past

Completed actions, repeated past actions, or past states.

She moved to Brazil in 1999.

Past Progressive

Actions actively in progress at a specific past time.

At 8 PM last night, I was studying.

Connecting with "When" and "While"

  • 1. Background Action Interrupted: Use Past Progressive for the background, Simple Past for the interruption.
    While I was eating, the phone rang.
  • 2. Two Actions in Progress: Use Past Progressive in both clauses.
    While she was reading, he was cooking.
  • 3. Sequential Events: Use Simple Past for both to show one happened right after the other.
    When the alarm went off, he woke up.

Writing Connection: "For example"

When providing supporting details, use commas correctly with for example:

  • Start of sentence: For example, many people own pets.
  • Middle of sentence: Many people, for example, own pets.
