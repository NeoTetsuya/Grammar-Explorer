Unit 6

Mastering Verb Forms

Discover when a verb acts as a noun (Gerund), when to use "to" + base verb (Infinitive), and the tricky verbs that completely change meaning depending on which form you choose.

1. Gerunds (Verb + ing)

Roles & Rules

  • As Subjects and Objects: A gerund acts as a noun. When it is the subject, it always takes a singular verb.
    Smoking is bad for you.
  • Negative Gerunds: Place not before the gerund.
    I recommend not eating late.

Objects of Prepositions

A gerund is the only verb form that can follow a preposition (in, on, at, about, for, of, by).

succeed in passing
believe in trying
afraid of failing
tired of waiting
advantage of studying
reason for leaving

Verbs & Expressions Followed by Gerunds

avoid consider dislike / enjoy finish / quit keep / mind practice / suggest

Special Noun/Time Expressions: have fun/trouble/experience, spend time/money, waste time/money.
"I spend time reading every night."

2. Infinitives (to + base verb)

Common Verb Patterns

  • Verb + Infinitive: afford, decide, hope, plan, promise, seem, want, would like.
    I hope to pass.
  • Verb + Object + Infinitive: advise, allow, convince, encourage, remind, tell, warn.
    He reminded me to call.

Subject "It" & Adjectives

When using the placeholder subject it, the infinitive phrase often goes at the end.

It + Verb/Adj + Infinitive: "It costs a lot to take the course."
"It is easy to make mistakes."
Attitude Adjectives: Adjectives showing feelings (glad, lucky, willing, afraid) precede an infinitive.
"I am glad to help."

Too and Enough

  • Too (Excessive): Comes before the adjective.
    He is too tired to study.
  • Enough (Sufficient): Comes after adjectives, but before nouns.
    Old enough to drive.

Infinitives of Purpose

Explain why someone does something.

Never use "for + gerund" to express purpose.
I study for learning.
Correct: I study to learn.

3. The Crossroads: Verbs taking both

No Change in Meaning

You can use either form after these verbs without changing the message: begin, (not) bother, can't stand, continue, hate, like, love, prefer, start.

"I love to swim" is the same as "I love swimming".

Drastic Change in Meaning

Stop
+ Gerund Ending an activity entirely.
"He stopped smoking." (He quit)
+ Infinitive Interrupting an action in order to do something else (purpose).
"He stopped to smoke." (He paused walking in order to smoke)
Remember
+ Gerund Recalling a memory of a past event.
"I remember locking the door." (I have a memory of doing it)
+ Infinitive Remembering a task you need to perform.
"I remembered to lock the door." (I didn't forget my obligation)
Forget
+ Gerund Losing the memory of a past event (usually used in negative).
"I will never forget visiting Paris."
+ Infinitive Failing to complete a required task.
"I forgot to buy milk." (I didn't do the chore)

Writing Connection: Comma Splices

A comma splice is a major error where two independent clauses (full sentences) are glued together with just a comma.

He loves swimming, he goes every day.
Fix 1: Conjunction → He loves swimming, so he goes every day.
Fix 2: Period → He loves swimming. He goes every day.
Fix 3: Semicolon → He loves swimming; he goes every day.
