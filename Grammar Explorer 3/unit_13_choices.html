1. Present & Future Real Conditionals

Real conditionals deal with factual situations, habits, or highly possible future outcomes.

Present Real

Facts, habits, and general truths. Uses the Simple Present in both clauses.

If I eat late, I don't sleep well.
Alternatives to "If"
  • Unless: Means "if... not."
    Unless I sleep, I feel tired.
  • Even if: Shows an unexpected result.
    Even if he sleeps, he is tired.
  • When: Can replace 'if' for habits.
    When he has time, he plays.

Future Real

Highly possible future outcomes. Uses Simple Present (If clause) and Will / Be going to (Main clause).

If he passes the exam, he will be happy.
Other Main Clause Forms
  • Modals: can, could, may, might, should, must.
    If it rains, you should stay inside.
  • Imperatives: Commands/Directions.
    If you go to the store, buy milk.
Punctuation Rule: If the If clause comes first, use a comma. If the main clause comes first, do not use a comma!

2. Present & Future Unreal Conditionals

Describe imaginary, untrue, or impossible scenarios right now or in the future.

If Clause Simple Past
Main Clause Would + Base Verb

The "Were" Rule

In formal English, always use were instead of was for all subjects (I, he, she, it) in the If clause.

"If I were rich, I would live in Paris."
Giving Advice The phrase "If I were you, I would..." is the standard, most polite way to give advice by imagining yourself in someone else's position.

Using "Wish" (Present/Future)

Use Wish + Simple Past to talk about situations you want to be different right now.

"I wish we lived in a nicer place."
Use could (not can) for desired abilities:
"I wish I could speak French."

3. Past Unreal Conditionals

Discuss hypothetical, counterfactual past conditions that did not actually happen.

If Clause Past Perfect (had + P.P.)
Main Clause Would have + P.P.

The Past Conditional

"If he had gone to class, he would have seen her."

Do not use the simple past in the If clause for past unreal situations!

Pronunciation

In speech, "would have" is often reduced to "would've" or informally "woulda" (sounding like "would of").

Using "Wish" (Past Regrets)

Use Wish + Past Perfect to express regrets about past events that did or did not happen.

"I wish my parents had encouraged me to take risks."
(Reality: They did not encourage me).
Short Answers "Would you have come?"
→ "No, I wouldn't have." (Do not repeat the past participle).

4. Syntactic Roles of Noun Clauses

A noun clause functions as a single noun entity within a sentence.

Subject Role

Subject:
• "Whatever option you choose will determine the outcome."
Subject Complement:
• "The main challenge is how we will fund the project."

Object & Appositive Roles

Direct Object:
• "She announced that the vote was unanimous."
Appositive:
• "The fact that temperatures are rising cannot be ignored."

Writing Connection: Avoiding Fragments

An If clause is a dependent clause. It cannot stand alone as a sentence. It must be attached to a main independent clause.

If I had known the truth. I would have stayed.
If I had known the truth, I would have stayed.
