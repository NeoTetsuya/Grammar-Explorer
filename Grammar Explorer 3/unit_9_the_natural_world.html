1. Active and Passive: The Basics

Transitive Verbs (Can be passive)

Only verbs that take a direct object (transitive verbs) can be made passive.

  • Active: Researchers discovered a new sea animal.
  • Passive: A new type of sea animal was discovered.

Intransitive Verbs (Cannot be passive)

Verbs that do not take a direct object (like disappear, happen, sleep) cannot be made passive.

  • Active: Those birds disappeared.
  • Incorrect Passive: Those birds are disappeared.

Passive Questions

Place the auxiliary verb (be or have) before the subject.

  • Were the applications completed?
  • Have the animals been trained?

Formal Register

The passive voice is a hallmark of formal, academic speaking and writing. It sounds more objective.

"Not a great deal is known about the ocean floor."

2. Passive with Modals

Present / Future Modal Passive

Modal (+ not) + be + Past Participle

  • Sea turtles must not be disturbed.
  • Rules should be followed.
  • No street lights will be turned on.
  • New beach rules are going to be posted.

Past Modal Passive

Modal (+ not) + have been + Past Participle

  • The money may have been taken.
  • She should not have been told the news.

3. The Agent: To "By" or Not To "By"?

Omission of the Agent

The person/thing doing the action is omitted if it is unknown, unimportant, or obvious.

African manatees can be found in western Africa.
(Obvious agent: by people / by scientists)
Never use vague agents!
Stolen by someone.
Released by people.

Using "By"

Include the agent with by when it introduces crucial new information, specific people, or specific numbers.

  • Specific Name: The portrait was painted by Picasso.
  • Specific Number: Read by millions of people.
  • Crucial Detail: Habitat is destroyed by the building of dams.

4. Advanced Forms: Gerunds, Infinitives, & Get

Passive Gerunds

being + Past Participle

Used after prepositions or verbs that take gerunds (mind, remember).

  • "They don't mind being asked."
  • "I remember being shown a bird's nest."

Passive Infinitives

to be + Past Participle

Used after adjectives, nouns, or verbs that take infinitives (expect, prepare).

  • "Areas that they expect to be affected."
  • "People prepare to be evacuated."

Get Passives (Informal)

get/got + Past Participle

In conversational English, "get" often replaces "be" in passive sentences.

  • "Information gets collected."
  • "They got sent to jail."

Writing Connection: Informative Essays

When writing an essay on a topic like endangered animals, the passive voice allows you to maintain an objective tone.

Application Strategy:
  • Omit the agent when stating general facts or locations.
    "The manatee is found in coastal areas." (Not: is found by people).
  • Use 'by + agent' only when the agent introduces essential, specific details.
    "Habitat is being destroyed by the building of dams."
