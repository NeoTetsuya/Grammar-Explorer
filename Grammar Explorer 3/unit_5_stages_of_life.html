1. Count & Non-Count Nouns

The Basics

  • Count Nouns Have singular and plural forms (e.g., one son, two sons).
  • Non-Count Nouns Cannot be counted. No plural forms. Take singular verbs. Do not use a/an (e.g., jewelry, homework, equipment).
  • Abstract Nouns (Usually Non-Count) Ideas (beauty, intelligence), Feelings (love, anger), Subjects (math, art), Weather (rain, fog).

Quantity Expressions

  • Any vs. No: Any + negative verb ("haven't made any"). No + affirmative verb ("have made no").
  • A Few vs. Few (Count): A few = positive amount (some). Few = negative amount (not many).
  • A Little vs. Little (Non-Count): A little = positive amount (some). Little = negative amount (not much).
  • Constraint: Much is NOT typically used in affirmative statements. Use a lot of instead.

Dual Meanings (Count vs. Non-Count)

Life Count: "He had a long life."
Non-Count: "Life is wonderful."
Glass Count: "A glass of water."
Non-Count: "Made of glass."
Beauty Count: "Your horse is a beauty."
Non-Count: "Beauty in nature."
Light Count: "Slept with a light on."
Non-Count: "Filled with light."
Appearance Count: "Made an appearance."
Non-Count: "Appearance is important."
Paper Count: "Write a paper."
Non-Count: "Made out of paper."
Room Count: "Share a room."
Non-Count: "Didn't have room for it."
Experience Count: "A scary experience."
Non-Count: "Experience with kids."

2. Articles: Specific, Nonspecific, & General

The (Definite)

Refers to a specific noun. It is clear which one you mean, or there is only one (e.g., the moon).

Look at the book that I bought.

A / An (Indefinite)

Used before singular count nouns when referring to a nonspecific item (one of many).

I want to buy a car.

Zero Article

No article is used in common fixed expressions.

to bed, in bed, in class, at work, at home

First vs. Subsequent Mention

I saw a dog. The dog was barking.

Use a/an for the first mention, and 'the' when mentioning it again.

Making Generalizations

  • Plural / Non-Count (Zero Article): "Cows are mammals." "Milk comes from cows." (Most common)
  • Singular Count (A/An): "A computer is a useful tool." (Less common)
  • The + Adjective: Used for specific groups (e.g., the poor, the elderly).

3. Advanced Subject-Verb Agreement

Verbs must agree with their subjects, but finding the true subject or knowing if it counts as singular or plural can be tricky.

Phrases & Conjunctions

  • Intervening Phrases: Verb agrees with the head noun, not the modifier.
    The girl with five brothers is athletic.
  • The "And" Conjunction: Usually plural. BUT, singular if preceded by each or every.
    Bob and Tom are here.
    Every day and night was fun.
  • Indefinite Pronouns (Singular):
    Everybody knows the answer.

Tricky Singulars

  • Amounts, Time, Distance: Treated as singular units.
    Two hundred dollars is a lot.
    Five miles isn't far.
  • Plural-Looking Nouns: Nouns ending in -ics or 'news'.
    Physics is hard. The news is good.
  • Proper Nouns with -s: Books, movies, countries.
    The United States is large.
There is / Here is Subject follows the verb.
"There are three dogs."
Number of... The number of = Singular
A number of = Plural
Fractions & Percentages Verb agrees with noun after 'of'.
"50% of the students know..."
"50% of the water is..."

Writing Connection: Narrative Sequencing & Fragments

  • Past Perfect Review: Use the past perfect (had + past participle) to clearly show that one action occurred before another past action in a story sequence.
  • Sentence Fragments: Avoid fragments! An if clause (e.g., "If I had known") is dependent and must always be attached to a main independent clause.
