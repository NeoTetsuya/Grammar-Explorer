Relative Clauses

Also known as adjective clauses, these powerful structures help you add descriptive details about people, places, and things.

Tap the dashed grammar words to reveal structural rules!

1. Subject Relative Clauses

In a subject relative clause, the relative pronoun functions as the subject of the clause. It directly performs the action.

The Pronouns

  • Who / That (People) "The photographer who won the award is here."
  • Which / That (Things/Animals) "There is an app that shows you how to edit."
    *Note: 'that' is more common than 'which' in conversation.
  • Whose (Possession) "The boy whose feet dangle over the water..."
    Never repeat the pronoun: "whose his feet"

Verb Agreement

The verb within the relative clause must agree in number with the noun it describes.

Singular Noun:
There is an app that shows...
Plural Noun:
There are apps that show...

Identifying vs. Non-Identifying Clauses

Identifying (Restrictive)

Provides essential information. Tells you exactly which person or thing we mean. No commas.

"My uncle who lives in New York owns a restaurant."
(Implies I have more than one uncle, and I am specifying which one).
Non-Identifying (Non-Restrictive)

Provides extra, non-essential information. Requires commas. You cannot use "that".

"My uncle, who lives in New York, owns a restaurant."
(Implies I only have one uncle; the location is just extra info).

2. Object Relative Clauses

The relative pronoun serves as the direct object of the verb within the clause.

Structure & Omission

You can use that, which, who, or whom (formal). The verb in the clause agrees with its own subject, not the modified noun.

The Omission Rule

In identifying clauses, you can drop the relative pronoun entirely.

"The man (that) she was interviewing is famous."

*You cannot omit the pronoun in non-identifying clauses.

Object Repetition Error

Do not repeat the object pronoun at the end of the clause.

Where's the man you were helping him?
Where's the man you were helping?

Clauses with Prepositions

Informal Usage

The preposition stays at the end of the clause. The pronoun is often omitted.

"The movie (that) we were talking about..."
Formal Usage

The preposition moves to the beginning of the clause. It MUST go before whom (people) or which (things).

"The article to which you are referring..."

You can never place a preposition directly before who or that (e.g., "to who" or "of that").

3. Reduced Clauses & Where/When

Reduced Relative Clauses

Subject relative clauses can be shortened into phrases.

  • With the verb "Be"

    Omit the relative pronoun and the "be" verb.

    "The girl who was riding the bike..." →
    "The girl riding the bike..."
  • With Active Verbs

    Omit the pronoun and change the verb to the -ing form.

    "Anyone who wants to learn..." →
    "Anyone wanting to learn..."

*Non-identifying reduced clauses must keep their commas.

Using Where and When

Relative adverbs used for places and times.

  • Where (Places)

    "Istanbul is a city where Europe and Asia meet."

    Formal alternative: "...a city in which Europe and Asia meet."

  • When (Times)

    "I’ll never forget the summer when our team won."

    Formal alternative: "...the summer in which our team won."

Preposition Warning: Never use a preposition directly before or inside a clause with where/when.
"the time during when..."
