Transitions & Prepositional Phrases

Elevate your academic writing by masterfully linking contrasting thoughts, showing logical results, adding supporting ideas, and explaining cause and effect.

1. Transition Words

Transition words connect ideas between or within sentences, heavily used in formal writing and speaking. Always follow them with a comma when starting a sentence.

Contrast

Similar meaning to but (introducing surprises or direct opposites).

  • However / Nevertheless: "The test was hard. However, everyone passed."
  • On the Other Hand: "The job pays well. On the other hand, it's boring."

Result

Similar meaning to so (introducing outcomes).

  • Therefore / Thus: "Pat missed the lecture. Therefore, he needs notes."
  • As a Result: "She has a great telescope. As a result, she saw Saturn."

Addition

Similar meaning to and (introducing supporting points).

  • In Addition / Moreover: "The essay should be clear. In addition, it must be engaging."

2. Prepositional Phrases & Intensifiers

Unlike transition words, these prepositions are followed by a noun phrase rather than a full clause.

Cause & Effect Prepositions

Followed by a noun phrase (e.g., *because of the storm* vs *because it stormed*).

  • As a result of / Because of / Due to:
    "Driving was difficult because of the snow."
  • Formal Option:
    "They left town due to the fact that a hurricane approached."

Contrast Prepositions

Followed by a noun phrase to show unexpected contrast.

  • Despite / In spite of:
    "Some passed despite the pressure."
  • Clause Alternative:
    "We went out in spite of the fact that it was stormy."

Expressing Extreme Cause & Effect: So / Such... That

Stating an extreme cause first, followed by the resulting consequence in a that-clause.

So + Adjective/Adverb + That "The exam was so easy that we finished early."
Such + Adjective + Noun + That "It was such an easy exam that I finished early."

Writing Connection: Using Semicolons

In persuasive or academic essays, you can link closely related independent clauses using a semicolon (;), often paired with a transition word like however or therefore.

Punctuation Pattern: Independent Clause ; Transition Word , Independent Clause
"The experiment failed; however, the team learned valuable data."
